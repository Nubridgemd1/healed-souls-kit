Scanxiety: Managing the Fear Between Cancer Scans and Appointments

The waiting between scans can be its own kind of hard. Learn practical, compassionate ways to manage scanxiety, and how Healed Souls supports emotional wellbeing through cancer care.
Share: WhatsApp Text Email Facebook X

There is a word that many people living with cancer come to know well: scanxiety. It describes the wave of fear and tension that builds in the days before a scan or a follow up appointment, and the anxious wait for results. If your heart races when a test is near, or your mind fills with worst case thoughts, you are not overreacting. This is one of the most common and most human parts of the cancer journey.

Why waiting feels so heavy

Scans carry enormous weight because so much can seem to hang on the outcome. The mind, trying to prepare for every possibility, often rehearses the worst one. Add a calendar full of appointments, past memories of hard news, and a body already tired from treatment, and it is no wonder the waiting feels overwhelming. Anxiety is the alarm system doing its job, even when there is nothing you can do in the moment but wait.

Grounding tools for the hardest days

You cannot always control the schedule or the results, but you can care for your mind while you wait:

  • Breathe slowly and deeply. A few minutes of slow breathing tells the body it is safe.
  • Return to the present. Name five things you can see, four you can hear, three you can touch.
  • Plan a comforting activity for scan day and results day, so you are not left alone with your thoughts.
  • Set limits on reassurance seeking and online searches, which often feed the fear.
  • Lean on prayer, scripture, music, or whatever steadies your spirit.

Give the worry a container

Many people find relief in setting aside a short, specific worry time rather than letting anxiety spill through the whole day. When fearful thoughts arrive outside that window, you can gently remind yourself that you will tend to them later. This is not denial. It is giving your mind a boundary so you can still live in the moments you have.

When anxiety needs more support

If scanxiety grows into panic attacks, sleepless nights that stretch on, or dread that never lifts between appointments, that is a sign to reach for help. Anxiety is highly treatable. Therapy, calming skills, and medication management when appropriate can quiet the alarm so you can breathe again.

If you are in crisis or having thoughts of harming yourself, you are not alone and help is available right now. Call or text 988 (the Suicide and Crisis Lifeline) or dial 911.
